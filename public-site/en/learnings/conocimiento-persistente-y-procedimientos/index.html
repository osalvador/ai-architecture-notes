The question connecting these readings

Section titled “The question connecting these readings”

What must an agent know to make a change, and what should remain available afterwards? The five readings contribute complementary pieces: intent, procedures, memory, boundaries and checks. Our proposed synthesis separates what to preserve, what to retrieve for the task and what to check when acting. This is an editorial interpretation reviewed by the owner; the authors have not evaluated this design together.

Preserving reasons gives the next change context

Section titled “Preserving reasons gives the next change context”

The Context Store article combines intent, behaviour and conformance alongside code through specifications, tests and fitness functions. In the “Takeaways” and “What?” sections of Canvas for AI Agents, Asthagiri proposes leaving decision records for whoever continues the work.

The connection is that an accepted result should leave a way to understand its purpose and the conditions of its decisions. When a constraint changes, we need to know which tests, procedures and decisions depended on it.

This does not require documenting everything. Where a small service already expresses contracts clearly, another store may duplicate information. Our proposed criterion is to preserve reasons someone else would otherwise have to reconstruct, with a current source for each.

Retrieving a procedure answers another question

Section titled “Retrieving a procedure answers another question”

In the LinkedIn transcript, Prakash explains task playbooks and progressive tool discovery. In “The Emergence of Agentic AI Systems” of his article, Gancarz distinguishes memory types and dynamic context management.

The editorial conclusion is that a contract and a procedure have different jobs. One explains what must remain true; the other guides how to proceed here. Both may be versioned and reach the same agent, without replacing one another.

Checking closes the loop

Section titled “Checking closes the loop”

In “Code: Execution as a First-Class Primitive” of The Evolution of Agentic Harnesses, Ramgopal describes generating, executing and revising code through feedback. Context Store incorporates tests and architecture controls; Canvas connects prior guidance and subsequent signals.

The proposed connection is that context also helps after a change: it allows comparison with intent and explanation of deviations. A passing test is evidence about a specific condition. When a requirement was misunderstood, the test may accept the wrong solution.

This is an editorial diagram. Reviewing artefacts requires judgement: a failure does not automatically make an agent output a valid new rule.

A fictional example

Section titled “A fictional example”

An agent changes retry behaviour in a service. A specification requires avoiding duplicate operations; a playbook explains environment setup; a test checks one repeated operation case. An obsolete playbook command calls for a procedure correction. A missing test case calls for more evidence. A changed business rule calls for reviewing intent and dependencies. These are different problems worth handling separately.

Conditions and open questions

Section titled “Conditions and open questions”

The combination requires owners, current references and useful checks. The sources offer different frameworks and experiences; they do not establish the need for a shared platform or that more context always improves results.

What information does the agent actually consult, how much maintenance is added and how many errors are avoided? Comparable tasks, correctness criteria fixed beforehand and records of human intervention would help evaluate the proposal. That evaluation has not been performed.
